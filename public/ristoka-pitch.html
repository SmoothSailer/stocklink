The Problem

Retailers are stuck in a cycle of scarcity

💰

No Working Capital

Retailers buy 2 bags when they could sell 10. Banks won't lend to them. M-Shwari caps at KSh 5,000. They need KSh 30,000–50,000/week.

🔗

Middleman Tax

No direct access to wholesalers or manufacturers. Every layer adds 10-20% markup. A KSh 3,200 bag of rice costs her KSh 3,800.

🌍

No Global Access

High-margin international products (snacks, cosmetics, electronics) are completely inaccessible. No logistics, no contacts, no capital.

BNPL Model

Murabaha — Transparent, Ethical Financing

🔍

KYC Verification

ID upload, business docs, admin review. Credit limit set per retailer.

📦

We Buy Inventory

Ristoka purchases stock at wholesale cost on behalf of the retailer.

💵

30% Down Payment

Retailer pays 30% upfront. Reduces our risk exposure immediately.

📅

Installments

Remaining 70% + markup spread over 2-4 weekly installments.

This is cost-plus, not interest. The markup is transparent and agreed upfront.
Shariah-compliant. Trust-building. Retailer-friendly.

Risk Mitigation

Built-in safeguards at every layer

  • 30% mandatory down payment — immediate capital recovery on every BNPL order
  • KYC verification — ID, business docs, admin review before credit access
  • Individual credit limits — set per retailer, tracked in real-time
  • Overdue detection — retailers with overdue installments are blocked from new BNPL
  • Inventory as collateral — worst case, stock is liquidatable physical goods
  • Short cycles — 2-4 week repayment windows, not months

Default Scenario

If a retailer defaults after paying 30% down on a KSh 35,000 order:

Down payment collected KSh 10,500
Max exposure KSh 24,500
Our cost was KSh 32,000

Risk buffer (5-10% of fund) covers this scenario.
